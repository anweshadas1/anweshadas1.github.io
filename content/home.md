## Research

I study what makes a machine (any AI system) **good** to work with. A large part of it, I think, is how the machine communicates with its human partner. My work draws on <span class="num">1</span>&nbsp;pragmatics in human–AI interaction and <span class="num">2</span>&nbsp;computational models of communication and attention, in the hope of <span class="num">3</span>&nbsp;more rational human–AI cooperation.

Much of my research so far has involved working with the [Rational Speech Act (RSA) framework](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S136466131630122X), a Bayesian model of pragmatic reasoning. My models extend it to [dynamic environments, where a speaker has to plan a whole sequence of messages](publications.html#planning-ahead). I also test them in behavioural experiments and see [whether people can recover meaning successfully](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03976) in time-critical settings.

For now, my machine speakers simply assume a listener's epistemic state. I would like them to learn from the context and adapt, perhaps from what a person does, or where they look. There are other (some related, some unrelated) directions that I am also interested in, and I would be glad of company in my quest to understand them: [attention-aware interfaces](publications.html#hceye), whether everyday AI assistants reason about their listener and vice versa, and what about the experiences of people who a system designed for the average, neurotypical person leaves out.
