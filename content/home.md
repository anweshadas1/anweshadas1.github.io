## Research

I study how AI systems should communicate with the people who depend on them, in time-critical settings where [attention is scarce](publications.html#hceye) and the situation keeps changing. I build [Bayesian models of pragmatic reasoning and planning](publications.html#planning-ahead), and test them in behavioural experiments with people. It comes back to three questions:

1. **Computational models of communication.** What should an assistive AI say, when should it say it, and how much can it leave for the human partner to fill in?
2. **Pragmatics in human–AI interaction.** To communicate well with humans, what must a machine believe about them: what a person knows? what they have noticed? and how can it learn this from their behaviour?
3. **Human–AI collaboration.** What makes a person and an assistant better together than either alone?

There are other (some related, some unrelated) directions that I am also interested in, and I would be glad of company in my quest to understand them: attention-aware interfaces, whether everyday AI assistants reason about their listener and vice versa, and what about the experiences of people who a system designed for the average, neurotypical person leaves out.
